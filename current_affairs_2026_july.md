## 2. 🏛️ REGULATORY BODIES NEWS

📰 **RBI Master Direction — Treatment of Wilful Defaulters and Large Defaulters (July 30)**

- **Scope & Applicability**: Applicable to all Scheduled Commercial Banks (incl. RRBs), All-India Financial Institutions (NABARD, EXIM, SIDBI, NHB), NBFCs, and Co-operative Banks.
- **Categorization Thresholds**:
  - **Wilful Defaulter**: Borrower or guarantor with an outstanding aggregate balance of **₹25 lakh and above**, who has defaulted despite having repayment capacity, siphoned/diverted funds, or disposed of charged assets without lender knowledge.
  - **Large Defaulter**: Borrower with an outstanding aggregate balance of **₹1 crore and above**, whose account is classified as Doubtful or Loss (or has been resolved via compromise/suit).
- **Mandatory Timelines for Lenders**:
  - Lenders must examine wilful default within **6 months of an account being classified as NPA**.
  - Identification Committee (Executive Director + two senior officials) issues a reasoned show cause notice allowing **21 days** for borrower response.
  - Review Committee (chaired by MD & CEO + two Independent Directors) confirms or cancels the classification.
- **Severe Regulatory Sanctions**:
  - **Credit Bar**: Barred from availing any fresh loan/credit facility from any institutional lender for **5 years** from the date their name is removed from the List of Wilful Defaulters.
  - **Corporate Bar**: Debarred from floating new ventures for 5 years and disqualified from serving as a Director on any corporate board under Section 164(2) of the Companies Act.
  - **Compromise Settlement Restriction**: No compromise or one-time settlement (OTS) allowed unless a mandatory cooling-off period of **12 months** has elapsed.

🎯 Exam Angle →

- Threshold Trap: Wilful Defaulter = **₹25 lakh and above**; Large Defaulter = **₹1 crore and above** (classic IBPS/RBI trap).
- Timeline Trap: Lender must initiate wilful default examination within **6 months of NPA classification** (not 1 year).
- Penal Debarment: Bar on new credit is **5 years** after removal of name; compromise settlement cooling-off is **12 months**.

📰 **RBI Master Directions on Fraud Risk Management in Regulated Entities (July 15)**

- **Scope**: Replaced the legacy 2016 framework; issued across three distinct directions for Commercial Banks (incl. RRBs), Co-operative Banks, and NBFCs.
- **Compliance with Supreme Court Mandate (*SBI vs. Rajesh Agarwal, 2023*)**:
  - Lenders must strictly adhere to the **Principles of Natural Justice** (*Audi Alteram Partem* — hear the other side).
  - Mandatory issuance of a detailed, reasoned **Show Cause Notice (SCN)** giving the borrower a minimum of **21 calendar days** to submit a written representation before classifying an account as 'Fraud'.
- **Board-Level Oversight (SCBF)**:
  - Mandatory constitution of a **Special Committee of the Board on Monitoring and Follow-up of Frauds (SCBF)**, comprising independent and non-executive directors.
  - Dedicated Fraud Monitoring Group (FMG) at the executive level.
- **Reporting & Registry Norms**:
  - Lenders must report fraud cases to the **Central Fraud Registry (CFR)** within **21 days of classification**.
  - Mandatory reporting to Law Enforcement Agencies (State Police / CBI) based on threshold: Frauds >₹3 crore by private/foreign banks to CBI/SFIO; PSU banks follow CVC guidelines.
- **Early Warning Signals (EWS)**: Mandatory implementation of 42 EWS indicators for all borrower credit exposures of **₹50 crore and above**.

🎯 Exam Angle →

- Landmark Precedent: Rooted in the Supreme Court judgment *State Bank of India & Ors. vs. Rajesh Agarwal & Ors.*
- Notice Window: Minimum **21 days** given to the borrower to reply to SCN before fraud classification.
- Reporting Deadline: Classification must be uploaded to RBI's Central Fraud Registry (CFR) within **21 days**.
- EWS Exposure Threshold: Mandatory for accounts **≥₹50 crore**.

📰 **RBI Prompt Corrective Action (PCA) Framework for Urban Co-operative Banks (UCBs)**

- **Regulatory Reach**: Extended to all Primary (Urban) Co-operative Banks categorized under **Tier 3 (deposits ₹100 crore to ₹1,000 crore)** and **Tier 4 (deposits >₹1,000 crore)**.
- **Three Core Financial Health Indicators**:
  1. **Capital Adequacy (CRAR)**: Mandatory minimum is 9% for Tier 3 UCBs and 12% for Tier 4 UCBs.
  2. **Asset Quality (Net NPA Ratio)**: Breaching Net NPA **> 6.0%** triggers Risk Threshold 1.
  3. **Profitability (Return on Assets - RoA)**: Negative RoA for two consecutive financial years triggers mandatory supervisory intervention.
- **Mandatory Corrective Invocations**:
  - **Risk Threshold 1**: Restrictions on dividend payout/profit distribution and mandatory capital restoration plan.
  - **Risk Threshold 2**: Complete ban on opening new branches, expansion of business lines, and restriction on capital expenditure.
  - **Risk Threshold 3**: Discretionary RBI powers to supersede the Board of Directors, enforce amalgamation/merger, or cancel banking license.

🎯 Exam Angle →

- Tier Applicability: Applicable only to **Tier 3 and Tier 4 UCBs** (deposits exceeding ₹100 crore); Tier 1 and Tier 2 UCBs are excluded.
- Net NPA Trigger: Net NPA crossing **6.0%** triggers PCA.
- Tier 4 Capital Rule: Tier 4 UCBs must maintain minimum CRAR of **12%** (vs. 9% for others).

📰 **RBI Revised Framework on Domestic Money Transfer (DMT) & Cash KYC (July 24)**

- **Context**: Comprehensive overhaul of domestic cash transfers through Business Correspondents (BCs), digital wallets, and prepaid payment instruments (PPIs) to curb illicit financial flows and mule accounts.
- **Mandatory Remitter Identification**:
  - Business correspondents and banking agents cannot execute cash-to-account remittances without **registering the remitter**.
  - Mandatory verification of remitter's mobile phone number using a one-time password (OTP) and validation of Officially Valid Documents (OVD) or Aadhaar e-KYC.
- **Transaction Limits Preserved**:
  - Maximum cash remittance limit remains **₹50,000 per month per remitter**.
  - Single cash transaction capped at **₹5,000** for non-KYC walk-in customers at BC outlets.
- **Beneficiary Verification**: Banks must include remitter identification details in the payment message (IMPS/NEFT/UPI) sent to the destination beneficiary bank to ensure end-to-end auditability.

🎯 Exam Angle →

- Monthly Cash Limit: ₹50,000 per remitter per month.
- Core Regulatory Mandate: Mandatory remitter mobile OTP verification and OVD registration for all cash remittances at BC points.

📰 **RBI Directives on Dividend Declaration by Commercial Banks**

- **Prudential Standards**: Revised framework specifying parameters commercial banks must satisfy to declare dividends on equity shares or remit profits (in the case of foreign bank branches).
- **Three Minimum Pre-requisites**:
  1. **CRAR**: Minimum Capital to Risk-Weighted Assets Ratio of at least **11.5%** (inclusive of 2.5% Capital Conservation Buffer) for each of the preceding three financial years.
  2. **Net NPA**: Net NPA ratio must be **less than 6.0%** for the financial year for which dividend is proposed.
  3. **Supervisory Compliance**: Bank must comply with RBI regulations on transfer to Statutory Reserve Fund (min 20% of net profits) and provisioning.
- **Dividend Payout Ratio Matrix**:
  - Net NPA = 0%: Maximum Dividend Payout Ratio is **50%**.
  - Net NPA between 0% and 1%: Maximum Dividend Payout Ratio is **40%**.
  - Net NPA between 5% and 6%: Maximum Dividend Payout Ratio is **10%**.

🎯 Exam Angle →

- Net NPA Ceiling: Must be **<6%** to be eligible for any dividend payout.
- Minimum CRAR: Must be at least **11.5%** for preceding 3 consecutive years.
- Maximum Payout Cap: **50%** of net profit (only for zero Net NPA banks).

📰 **Trade Receivables Discounting System (TReDS) & Mandatory CERSAI Registration**

- **Statutory Framework**: Governed under the Payment and Settlement Systems Act, 2007, and the Factoring Regulation Act, 2011, providing non-recourse digital discounting of MSME trade receivables.
- **Mandatory CERSAI Registration**:
  - All Factoring Units (invoices/bills of exchange) created and financed on TReDS platforms must be registered with **CERSAI** (Central Registry of Securitisation Asset Reconstruction and Security Interest of India).
  - Registration prevents fraudulent dual-financing or pledging of the same receivable across multiple commercial lenders.
- **Operating Platforms in India**:
  - **RXIL** (Receivables Exchange of India Ltd — India's first operational TReDS exchange launched in 2016; joint venture of NSE and SIDBI).
  - **M1xchange** (Mynd Solutions Pvt. Ltd.).
  - **Invoicemart** (A.TREDS Ltd. — Axis Bank & mjunction).
  - **C2treds** (C2FO Factoring Solutions Pvt. Ltd.).
  - **DTX** (KredX Platform Pvt. Ltd.).
- **Net Worth Mandate**: Existing RBI-authorized TReDS operators must meet the statutory minimum net worth requirement of **₹25 crore** by **March 31, 2027**.
- **Eligible Participants**: Buyers (corporates, CPSEs, government departments), Sellers (MSMEs), and Financiers (banks, NBFC factors).

🎯 Exam Angle →

- Fraud Prevention: Mandatory registration with **CERSAI** eliminates duplicate invoice financing.
- First TReDS Platform: **RXIL** (commenced operations in 2016; NSE + SIDBI JV).
- Net Worth Deadline: Existing operators must achieve **₹25 crore net worth by 31 March 2027**.
- Ineligible Participants: Stock exchanges and insurance underwriters cannot participate as financiers on TReDS.

📰 **Historic Legal Transition: Three New Criminal Laws Enacted Nationwide (July 1)**

- **Enforcement Date**: Three transformative criminal law statutes came into full legal effect across India on **July 1, 2024**, completely repealing and replacing British colonial-era penal codes:
  1. **Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita, 2023 (BNS)**: Replaced the Indian Penal Code, 1860 (IPC).
  2. **Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita, 2023 (BNSS)**: Replaced the Code of Criminal Procedure, 1973 (CrPC).
  3. **Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam, 2023 (BSA)**: Replaced the Indian Evidence Act, 1872.
- **Key Judicial Innovations**:
  - Mandates **Zero FIR** (can be filed at any police station irrespective of jurisdiction).
  - Introduction of **e-FIR** and compulsory electronic summons via SMS/WhatsApp.
  - Mandatory audio-video recording of search and seizure operations, and mandatory forensic investigation for offences punishable by **7 years or more**.
  - Introduction of **Community Service** as a recognized formal penal punishment for minor offences.

🎯 Exam Angle →

- Enforcement Date: **July 1, 2024** (not Jan 1 or April 1).
- Statutory Cross-Mapping: BNS = IPC; BNSS = CrPC; BSA = Evidence Act.
- Forensic Threshold: Mandatory for offences carrying imprisonment of **7 years or more**.

📰 **National Statistics Day (June 29) & P.C. Mahalanobis Legacy**

- **Commemorative Observance**: Celebrated annually on **June 29** (designated in 2007) honoring the birth anniversary of **Prof. Prasanta Chandra Mahalanobis**, universally recognized as the *"Father of Indian Modern Statistics"*.
- **2024 National Theme**: *"Use of Data for Decision Making"*.
- **Foundational Institutional & Policy Contributions**:
  - Established the **Indian Statistical Institute (ISI)** in Kolkata in 1931.
  - Chief architect of India's **Second Five-Year Plan (1956–1961)**, based on the **Nehru-Mahalanobis Strategy** of rapid industrialization through heavy basic and capital goods industries.
  - Formulated the **Mahalanobis Distance**, a fundamental statistical measure used in multivariate data analysis.
- **National Recognition**: Ministry of Statistics and Programme Implementation (MoSPI) confers the annual **Prof. P.C. Mahalanobis National Award in Official Statistics** to eminent statisticians.

🎯 Exam Angle →

- Five-Year Plan Model: **Second Five-Year Plan (1956–61)** was modeled on the Mahalanobis strategy.
- Premier Institution: Founded **Indian Statistical Institute (ISI)** in Kolkata (1931).

## 10. 📌 MISCELLANEOUS — GOVT SCHEMES & STATIC

📰 **Prudential Guidelines on Project Finance — Construction Phase Debt (RBI Exposure Draft)**

- **Context**: Standard prudential guidelines for financing infrastructure and non-infrastructure project loans during the pre-commissioning phase.
- **Graduated Provisioning Architecture**:
  - **Construction Phase (Under-Construction)**: Proposed standard asset provisioning stepped up to **5.0%** (up from current baseline of 0.4%) in a phased manner (2% by FY25, 3.5% by FY26, 5% by FY27).
  - **Operational Phase (Post-DCCO)**: Once the project achieves Date of Commencement of Commercial Operations (DCCO), provisioning drops to **2.5%**.
  - **Stabilization Phase**: Drops further to **1.0%** once the project generates sufficient positive free cash flow and repays at least 20% of its long-term debt.
- **Financial Closure Deadline**: Financial closure must be achieved within **1 year** from the date of sanction for infrastructure projects.

🎯 Exam Angle →

- Proposed Peak Provisioning: **5.0%** during construction phase (vs. normal 0.4%).
- Operational Step-Down: Drops to **2.5%** upon achieving DCCO, and **1.0%** upon 20% debt repayment.
